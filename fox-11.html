Fox Nine · 50 movement and rig checks

These are the movement systems present in this build, plus the corrections made in this pass. A checklist is not a substitute for motion capture or a final animation review.

Foot contact and support

  1. Run the simulation on a 120 Hz fixed step so paw contacts and springs do not change with display refresh rate.
  2. Cap accumulated frame time after a hitch so returning to the tab cannot launch the fox through the level.
  3. Move the fox by velocity integration rather than frame-sized position jumps.
  4. Use acceleration ramps for starts instead of instantly reaching top speed.
  5. Use a distinct, stronger braking response when input reverses direction.
  6. Delay the facing flip until the body has begun turning or nearly stopped.
  7. Keep the turn duration proportional to current speed so fast reversals take longer.
  8. Use exponential drag while releasing controls for a smooth, tunable stop.
  9. Use stronger grounded drag than airborne drag to preserve momentum in the air.
  10. Clamp running and stalking speeds separately so stealth motion cannot inherit full sprint speed.

Stride and gait timing

  1. Advance the gait from distance traveled so stride timing follows actual speed.
  2. Keep the walk as a four beat sequence with separate offsets for each leg.
  3. Blend toward a diagonal trot continuously instead of switching gait phases abruptly.
  4. Blend toward a gathered gallop at high speed with a separate fast-gait phase.
  5. Give the hind limbs a drive phase before the forelimb reach in the fast gait.
  6. Lengthen the stride gradually with speed instead of scaling it in one jump.
  7. Shorten forelimb reach in the gallop so the shoulder does not overextend.
  8. Use longer ground support at walking speed and shorter support during a bound.
  9. Ease the stance-to-swing timing around phase boundaries.
  10. Use a curved toe-clearance arc so paws do not move linearly through the ground.

Leg rig and planted paws

  1. Keep upper and lower limb lengths fixed while solving the knees and hocks with two-bone IK.
  2. Reserve a little reach at full extension so joints do not lock straight.
  3. Clamp unreachable paw targets before solving the limb.
  4. Choose a consistent joint bend direction for forelegs and hindlegs.
  5. Anchor a paw in world space during the loaded part of stance to reduce foot skating.
  6. Release the planted paw when the gait enters swing or the fox leaves the ground.
  7. Release a planted paw before the leg reaches its extension limit.
  8. Fade contact weight in smoothly instead of snapping between free and planted poses.
  9. Keep each far-side leg on its own phase and draw it behind the near-side leg.
  10. Separate standing paw offsets so the far hind foot remains visible behind the near one.

Spine, head and weight

  1. Give the rib cage and pelvis separate damped spring channels instead of rotating the whole torso as one block.
  2. Blend the waist between rib and pelvis motion to keep the topline continuous.
  3. Delay the shoulder response behind a turn so the head leads and the pelvis follows.
  4. Use acceleration lean on takeoff and braking lean when the fox decelerates.
  5. Drive pelvic compression from the loaded hind stride rather than adding constant bounce.
  6. Keep torso bounce tied to the gait phase and reduce it when the fox slows.
  7. Use different rise and tuck poses for takeoff, apex and descent.
  8. Blend a landing impact into recovery rather than stopping the body instantly.
  9. Stabilize the head against rib motion with a damped counter-motion channel.
  10. Let ears lag acceleration and landing forces with their own spring and damping.

Tail, jump and recovery

  1. Attach the tail root inside the pelvis silhouette so the torso overlaps the seam.
  2. Move the tail root with the pelvis instead of anchoring it to the world.
  3. Use a broad brush profile that thickens through the middle and tapers at the tip.
  4. Carry the pale tip along the tail geometry so it bends with the brush.
  5. Keep a relaxed downward tail angle at rest and reduce idle wag amplitude.
  6. Hold the tail closer to horizontal at speed so it counterbalances the running body.
  7. Propagate tail motion from the base toward the tip with progressively softer segments.
  8. Increase tail damping after turns and landings so it settles instead of oscillating.
  9. Use jump anticipation and coyote time so takeoff input feels deliberate and forgiving.
  10. Shape a landing recoil and recovery, and keep the head and tail responding to the impact.
Fox Nine · leg anatomy pass and second review

Implemented from shoulder and hip through elbow, stifle, hock, wrist, and paw. The list describes actual changes in this duplicate, not a wish list.

Structure and proportions

  1. Removed the oversized 8.5–22.5 px hidden toe offsets that visually lengthened the legs below the hock.
  2. Rebalanced upper and lower limb lengths so the bones now span the grounded body height without oversized feet.
  3. Gave forelegs a longer, straighter support line and hind legs a stronger upper thigh.
  4. Moved the fore shoulder and hind hip roots into clearer anatomical positions.
  5. Set the fore elbow to fold backward and the hind stifle to fold forward in the side view.
  6. Kept a small extension reserve to prevent locked, ruler-straight knees.
  7. Reduced excessive knee compression while preserving a natural folded range.
  8. Made upper limbs fuller near the shoulder/hip and tapered them toward the joint.
  9. Made forearm and lower hind limb slimmer than the proximal muscle groups.
  10. Kept the far-side limbs slimmer and darker to reinforce depth.
  11. Re-shaped the hindquarter muscle mass to flow from rump into femur and stifle.
  12. Rounded the shoulder, elbow, stifle, wrist, and hock transitions so segments read as one connected limb.
  13. Removed hard bead-like joint marks and replaced them with small integrated forms.
  14. Added subtle directional muscle highlights that follow the femur.
  15. Kept the lower limbs dark like fox stockings while preserving coat color on proximal limbs.

Feet, stance, and gait

  1. Rebuilt paws as compact canine shapes with a clear heel and forward-pointing toes.
  2. Separated four toe shapes with understated furrow lines.
  3. Added tiny, low-contrast claw tips instead of chair-foot silhouettes.
  4. Scaled front paws slightly fuller than hind paws.
  5. Added subtle paw pitch: relaxed during stance, flexed during swing.
  6. Reduced swing height so feet skim naturally instead of paddling.
  7. Balanced hind reach against fore reach through the stride cycle.
  8. Lengthened the grounded support phase at walk and shortened it into the gallop.
  9. Kept planted paws world-locked while the body travels over them.
  10. Preserved the IK two-bone solve while leaving flex at full extension.
  11. Used a smooth tapered bone contour instead of angular limb polygons.
  12. Kept limb squash out of the skeleton so bone lengths remain stable.
  13. Maintained far-limb/body/near-limb draw order for overlap and readable depth.
  14. Reduced left-right paw spacing in the idle stance so all four legs can be read.
  15. Lowered idle toes close to the foot line for a quiet, weight-bearing pose.
  16. Smoothed paw lift and touchdown through the existing phase-continuous gait curves.
  17. Kept the back leg drive and foreleg catch distinct in the running cycle.
  18. Preserved body momentum, jump tuck, and landing recovery while refining the leg rig.

Second-pass critique

The dangling-foot proportion is corrected, the hindquarter now carries visible muscle, and the paw reads as a small canine foot instead of a flat stool. The legs still need a visual pass at full-size and during slow turns: a single side-view rig cannot show the far-side joints independently, and the paws remain a stylized four-toe shorthand. The next highest-value refinement would be a front three-quarter view or a short frame-by-frame gait review, especially through walk-to-gallop transitions and landing. This pass improves the rig, but it does not claim biological motion capture.
