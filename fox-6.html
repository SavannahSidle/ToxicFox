Fox Six critic pass · 50 body and motion refinements

Body silhouette and head

  1. Lower the back line into the shoulder instead of lifting the chest like a proud show dog.
  2. Give the rib cage a deeper oval while keeping the waist narrow.
  3. Separate the rib cage from the pelvis with a visible flexible loin.
  4. Round the rump into the hip rather than ending the back in a flat wedge.
  5. Carry the haunch volume into the upper hind leg.
  6. Keep the hindquarter mass behind the ribs instead of enlarging the whole body.
  7. Lift the belly line toward the groin for a runner's tuck.
  8. Make the chest deeper at the sternum than at the flank.
  9. Taper the abdomen from ribs to pelvis instead of using a uniform sausage.
  10. Keep the body long and low relative to its leg height.
  11. Blend neck into shoulder with an oblique slope.
  12. Overlap the skull and neck so no seam reads as a collar.
  13. Lower the head in neutral idle and during cautious movement.
  14. Keep the crown compact so the ears remain the highest alert points.
  15. Refine the cheek into a fox wedge instead of a round dog head.
  16. Lengthen the muzzle taper toward the nose without making it needle-thin.
  17. Separate upper bridge, whisker pad, lower jaw, and nose planes.
  18. Keep the cream bib attached to chest anatomy as the spine bends.
  19. Add a soft scapular landmark to show the shoulder sliding under skin.
  20. Use fur shading to describe muscle planes rather than painted stripes.

Skeleton, limbs, and paw contact

  1. Make the hip joint line up with the pelvis pivot.
  2. Keep forelimb roots under the moving scapula, not rigidly pinned to the torso.
  3. Give the hind thigh a fuller wedge and a clear stifle.
  4. Place the elbow below the shoulder and direct it backward.
  5. Set the carpus lower than the elbow with a clean, slim forearm.
  6. Show the hock as a rear-facing hinge, not a second knee.
  7. Preserve limb segment lengths through every pose.
  8. Keep far legs visibly behind the trunk and slightly darker.
  9. Increase paw volume enough to read as paws at gameplay scale.
  10. Separate toes and claws with only a few fine marks.
  11. Place feet under the center of mass while stopped.
  12. Keep the stance wide enough for balance, never splayed like a plush toy.
  13. Plant each foot in world space through its stance phase.
  14. Release foot locks at the end of stance rather than letting paws slide.
  15. Lift each paw on a smooth arc with faster toe clearance.
  16. Let the toes lead during swing and settle heel-first into contact.

Gait and body physics

  1. Use a four-beat walk with alternating diagonal support.
  2. Keep trot diagonal pairs synchronized and stable.
  3. Gather the hind legs before the gallop push.
  4. Give the gallop a readable compression, extension, and brief flight.
  5. Load the shoulders before takeoff and extend the spine after the push.
  6. Flex the lumbar spine between ribs and pelvis rather than bending as one block.
  7. Delay rib motion behind the pelvis to show elastic mass.
  8. Stabilize the head against body bounce while allowing small lag.
  9. Lean the body into acceleration and settle forward over planted paws when braking.
  10. Catch landings on forelimbs, then let the hindquarters follow.

Tail shape and balance

  1. Keep the tail thick at its base and taper it toward a rounded brush tip.
  2. Shape the tail as a soft brush tube rather than a flat triangular flag.
  3. Let tail segments follow with spring lag from base to tip.
  4. Use the tail as a counterweight in turns, jumps, and hard landings.
